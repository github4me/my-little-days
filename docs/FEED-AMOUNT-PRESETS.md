# Age-aware feed amount shortcuts

The feed editor adapts its four **formula-recording shortcuts** to the baby's age
on the feed's start date, using the saved birth date. These are convenience buttons,
not recommended doses, minimums, maximums, or a feeding plan.

| Age on feed date | Quick amounts (mL) |
| --- | --- |
| First week (0–6 completed days) | 15, 30, 45, 60 |
| 7 days to under 1 calendar month | 30, 60, 90, 120 |
| 1 to under 6 calendar months | 60, 90, 120, 150 (existing choices) |
| 6 to under 12 calendar months | 120, 150, 180, 240 |
| 12 months onward, unknown/invalid age, or expressed milk | 60, 90, 120, 150 (existing choices) |

Month anniversaries clamp to the last day of shorter months. Calendar-day
arithmetic avoids daylight-saving shifts. Historical feeds use historical age,
not today's age. No corrected-age or weight-based dose is inferred.

The app never changes the entered amount when the date, age band, or feed type
changes. The existing new-feed value of 120 mL remains unchanged. Any valid actual
amount can still be entered; the Stop confirmation wheel and saving rules are
unchanged. Direct breastfeeding remains duration-only.

## Sources and limits

Reviewed 13 September 2026:

- [American Academy of Pediatrics: Amount and Schedule of Baby Formula Feedings](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/formula-feeding/Pages/amount-and-schedule-of-formula-feedings.aspx)
  describes broad formula-feeding ranges of about 30–60 mL in the first week,
  increasing toward 90–120 mL during the first month, and around 180–240 mL at
  6 months. The exact four-button sets and age boundaries above are **product
  choices**, not an AAP dosing schedule. Smaller shortcuts permit recording
  partial feeds without treating a full bottle as a target.
- [CDC: How Much and How Often to Feed Infant Formula](https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/formula-feeding/how-much-and-how-often.html)
  emphasizes individual needs and hunger/fullness cues. Age alone cannot determine
  a baby's correct intake; changes with complementary foods also vary.
- [NHS: Bottle feeding advice](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/breastfeeding-and-bottle-feeding/bottle-feeding/advice/)
  recommends responsive feeding and not forcing a baby to finish a bottle.

Age-adjusted buttons include a bilingual explanation and a link to the AAP source.
The formula-specific ranges are not applied to expressed milk. Families should
follow their clinician's individual plan where one is provided.
